Why we built this

The archive is open.
Attention is the work.

Every major museum now publishes its collection online, openly, for nothing. And almost nobody looks. Not because people don't care — because nothing asks them to. The Long Look is a small answer to that: a personal practice of looking, one work at a time.

The purpose

A practice, not a feed

Most art apps are built to keep you scrolling. This one is built to make you stop. You tell us a variation, a date, and a time. At that moment, one work arrives in your inbox — with its title, its maker, its date, its medium, and a few prompts for looking at it properly.

That's the whole product. One work. One sitting. No infinite scroll, no algorithm, no "you might also like". The constraint is the point.

We think a person who spends fifteen deliberate minutes with six works a month will get more out of art than someone who saves two hundred images they'll never open again. This isn't about going anywhere — it's about building a private practice you actually keep.

The theme

Colour palette

The site is built on a four-colour palette — warm gallery walls, deep navy shadows, terracotta, and soft peach. These are the exact tokens used across every page.

What it does

Four moving parts

Everything below runs on public art data and a single email service. No accounts, no tracking, no ads.

◐

Draws from open archives

We query the Art Institute of Chicago's open API — over a hundred thousand works, all free, no key required. Every work you see is a real record, credited in full.

◑

Maps variations to searches

"Watercolor Landscapes" becomes watercolor landscape. "Charcoal Sketching" becomes charcoal. Six variations, six curated paths into the archive.

◒

Sends on your schedule

You pick the date and the time. We build the reminder, attach the work, add a materials list tailored to your setup, and email it the moment you asked for it.

◓

Keeps your study list

Reminders you've set live in a dashboard on your device. Over a few weeks you build a small private collection of works you've genuinely sat with.

Who it's for

Four kinds of people

The practising artist

You want reference and rigour, not inspiration quotes. Each reminder arrives with a materials list for your medium and prompts that push you past the first obvious reading of the work.

The curious beginner

You like art but don't know where to start. The variation names are plain English, the prompts assume nothing, and there's no wrong way to do this.

The self-taught learner

You're teaching yourself and want a steady, low-friction habit that doesn't depend on anyone else's curriculum. Set it once, and it keeps showing up.

The over-scroller

You save hundreds of images and revisit none. This is the opposite: a small number of works, looked at properly, on a schedule you set.

What we won't do

Three rules

01

No accounts

We don't want your password, your profile, or your birthday. A name and an email is the whole ask.

02

No tracking

No analytics pixels, no ad partners, no behavioural profiling. Your study list stays on your device.

03

No infinite scroll

You get one work per reminder. We will never send you a "while you're here" second one.
